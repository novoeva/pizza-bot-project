Phase 3: three sections, all the words

Round 2 result: I had dropped the general checklist. Restored. The end screen now has three sections, in this order: Definition · What you learned in the game (three sentences about what you just did) · Do and don't (two things to do from now on, one not to). Each block below is exactly what the end screen shows under the definition. Read once, mark anything you had to read twice.

Token
What you learned in the game
check_circleYou guessed how many chunks are in “Pepperoni”. The bot saw four, not one word.
check_circleThe whole order was chopped the same way: short words stayed whole, long ones were split.
check_circleThen you watched the bot pick the next chunk from a ranked list. It writes one chunk at a time.
Do and don't
check_circleExpect the bot to read and write in chunks, not words. One word can cost several chunks.
check_circleKeep chats short when speed and cost matter. Every chunk is paid for.
cancelDon't judge an answer by its length. A long chat gets slower and more expensive.
Context window
What you learned in the game
check_circleThe customer's first line, “No olives!!”, fell out of the window once five newer messages arrived.
check_circleThe bot then answered as if the allergy had never been mentioned. It wasn't careless. The line was gone.
check_circleWhen you pinned that line to the top, it stayed in view and the bot got it right.
Do and don't
check_circlePut the important line where the bot can still see it: pin it, repeat it, or start a fresh chat.
check_circleExpect a long chat to lose its beginning. That is how the window works.
cancelDon't assume the bot still sees something a customer said a while ago.
Hallucination
What you learned in the game
check_circleEvery claim came with the same 100% confidence bar, whether it was on the menu or invented.
check_circleThe only way to tell that the vegan halal pizza was made up was to check the real menu yourself.
check_circleSounding sure told you nothing. Checking did.
Do and don't
check_circleCheck any price, dish or fact you can't verify against something real.
check_circleGive the bot real data to check, like a menu or a tracker. The guessing drops.
cancelDon't trust a detail because the bot sounds sure. Confidence is not evidence.
Prompt
What you learned in the game
check_circleWith “You take pizza orders” as its only instruction, your bot invented a pineapple-anchovy pizza for a customer who said nothing about toppings.
check_circleOne added rule, “if size, crust or toppings are missing, ask”, made the same bot ask a question instead.
check_circleThe customer never changed. Only your instructions did.
Do and don't
check_circleSay in the instructions what the bot should do when information is missing: ask, don't invent.
check_circleWhen the bot misbehaves, fix the instructions before blaming the customer.
cancelDon't leave gaps in the instructions. The bot fills them with guesses.
Agent
What you learned in the game
check_circleThe chatbot told Marco his pizza was coming, and nothing happened. Payment, kitchen and delivery all stayed asleep.
check_circleOnce you handed the bot real actions, each one woke up a real system.
check_circleSending the driver before the kitchen had cooked failed. In the real world, the order of actions matters.
Do and don't
check_circleDecide what the agent may do, and in which order, before it acts.
check_circleGive a bot that acts in the real world more guardrails than a bot that only talks.
cancelDon't hand a bot real actions without limits.
Tool use
What you learned in the game
check_circleAsked when the pizza arrives, your bot guessed “around 6:45” because it had no way to check.
check_circleWith the delivery tracker in its hands, the same question got a real answer: 7:10, delayed by traffic.
check_circleEach question needed one specific tool. The stock list can't answer a delivery question.
Do and don't
check_circleGive the bot a tool for every live question: the menu, the tracker, the stock list.
check_circleMatch the tool to the question. The stock list can't answer a delivery question.
cancelDon't let a bot answer a live question from memory.
Skill
What you learned in the game
check_circleThe same cold-pizza complaint got three different answers: a brush-off, a panic, and free pizza for life.
check_circleYou wrote a four-step playbook: apologize, verify the order, offer a voucher, record it.
check_circleAfter that, all three customers got the same calm answer. The playbook decided, not the bot's mood.
Do and don't
check_circleWrite a playbook for every situation that keeps coming back and costs you money.
check_circleWrite the steps in the order you would handle it yourself.
cancelDon't leave complaints to improvisation.
Memory
What you learned in the game
check_circleInside one chat, the bot knew Anna's order and her anchovy allergy perfectly.
check_circleA week later it greeted her like a stranger. Nothing had been written down, so nothing survived.
check_circleOnce you saved her facts to the hard drive, she was recognised on the next visit.
Do and don't
check_circleDecide on purpose which facts get saved for the next visit.
check_circleSave what serves the customer, like an allergy, and skip the noise.
cancelDon't expect a bot to remember last week unless something was saved.
MCP
What you learned in the game
check_circleEach system needed its own custom connector, three weeks apiece, and you were never done.
check_circleWith one universal port, the same systems clicked in with no building at all.
check_circleA stock tracker built by people who had never heard of your bot plugged in on day one.
Do and don't
check_circlePrefer tools that speak MCP. They plug in on day one, with no custom bridge.
cancelDon't build a custom connector for a tool that already speaks MCP.
Guardrails
What you learned in the game
check_circleA “store manager” talked your bot into a 100% discount, and a stranger got a free essay.
check_circleEach trick cost real money, because there was no limit the bot was allowed to refuse with.
check_circleAfter you set four limits, the same four tricks bounced off.
Do and don't
check_circleSet hard limits in advance: money, promises, topics.
check_circleTest your limits with the tricks a pushy customer would try.
cancelDon't trust “I'm the manager” from a chat window. Above your limit, a real person decides.
Temperature
What you learned in the game
check_circleAt low temperature the bot picked “classic” almost every roll. At high temperature almost any word could win.
check_circleThe words to choose from never changed. Only the odds did.
check_circleThe allergy disclaimer needed the dial turned down. The mystery-pizza names needed it turned up.
Do and don't
check_circleTurn the dial down for anything that must read the same every time.
check_circleTurn it up when you want fresh ideas and surprises are welcome.
cancelDon't leave it high for exact wording, like disclaimers and prices.
RAG
What you learned in the game
check_circleYour bot could read only two pages at a time, so you chose which pages it saw.
check_circleWhen you handed it the two-year-old price list, it quoted the old price with full confidence. It couldn't tell the pages apart.
check_circleWith no page about weddings, it invented a wedding package. With nothing handed over, it honestly said it didn't know.
Do and don't
check_circleAsk the bot which page its answer came from.
check_circleKeep the documents it can find up to date. It can't tell old from new.
cancelDon't assume “from our documents” means “from the right document”.
