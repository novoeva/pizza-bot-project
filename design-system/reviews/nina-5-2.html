Nina interview · section 5 of 9 · point 5.2 · round 1

The bot picks the next token, and the screen says so

What Nina hit: the second part of the game has the bot predict a whole word. But a model never predicts a whole word, it predicts a token. She assumed "pizza" is at least two tokens, so the screen contradicted what she had just learned.

What is actually true

In this game "pizza" is one token: part 1 already shows the order "I'd like a pepperoni pizza!" chopped with "pizza" as a single piece. Short, common words are usually one token. So the bot really is picking the next token here. The problem was that the screen never said that, and one option ("salami") would in fact split into pieces.

What changed (3 things)

  1. The instruction says it outright: "Each option below is one token: short, common words like these are a single token each." The bracketed "(an LLM, short for large language model)" is gone.
  2. The question above the options says "Which token comes next? Each option is one token."
  3. "salami" is replaced by "salad", a word that is one token, so no option quietly breaks the rule.
